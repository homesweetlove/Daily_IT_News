## IT News

### 1. Microsoft 365 incident marked restored, with some residual recovery work still noted

**What's new:** The November 14 entry for Microsoft incident **MO1550821** showed the status `serviceRestored`. According to a public University of Pennsylvania copy of the Microsoft admin-center notice, the final explanation was posted on November 14 at 04:17 UTC, and the record's last update was at 14:24 UTC that day. This was a subsequent development following the previous day's degradation. [Public copy of the Microsoft notice](https://mailservices.isc.upenn.edu/computing/email/penno365/alerts/ms-incidents.html)

**Key details:** Microsoft attributed the incident to a problem involving an earlier network-capacity expansion that surfaced after a more recent network change. It stated that the underlying issue had been addressed and that services were progressively restored as stability was verified. The notice said the issue was unrelated to a cyberattack, AI, or agents. Exchange Online and functions that depend on email were affected. A full post-incident report was to follow review. [Public copy of the Microsoft notice](https://mailservices.isc.upenn.edu/computing/email/penno365/alerts/ms-incidents.html)

**Why it matters:** Despite the restored status, the same final explanation mentioned continuing work to restore service for remaining customers and regions. That does not prove every function in every organization recovered at the same moment. **What to watch:** Administrators should test sending and receiving mail, delayed messages, and email-dependent automation; they should also identify jobs that need to be retried. Detailed root-cause and preventive-action information will require the post-incident report. These are editorial checks derived from the [notice](https://mailservices.isc.upenn.edu/computing/email/penno365/alerts/ms-incidents.html).

### 2. DeepSeek expands its developer APIs to search, documents, and voice

**What's new:** November 14 reporting covered application APIs that DeepSeek announced in its **November 13 changelog**. Official documentation states that `deepseek-flash` supports web search, file input, and URL reading through the Messages, Responses, and Chat Completions formats. [Official changelog](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/) · [November 14 report](https://neuralbrew.ai/en/articles/2026/11/deepseek-api-app-features-web-search-voice/)

**Key details:** Web search can return answers with citations. With reasoning enabled, a model can perform multi-step searches and read linked pages. PDFs, Office documents, and images pass through text extraction or document-structure analysis before being added to the conversation context. Search, page fetching, OCR, speech recognition, and speech synthesis are also exposed as independent APIs. The service handles tool use and context assembly. [Official changelog](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/)

**Why it matters:** Developers may need less custom orchestration to combine search, document processing, and voice. **What to watch:** Before adopting the APIs, test citation accuracy on Korean-language documents, table and layout extraction, latency, usage-based costs, and what information leaves your system for the external API. These are editorial evaluation criteria, **not claims that quality or performance gains have been independently verified**. [Official feature list](https://api-docs.deepseek.com/updates/)

## Security News

### 1. Exploited UnrealIRCd command-injection flaw calls for checks across connected servers

**What's new:** According to November 14 coverage, CISA added **CVE-2026-114713** to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog on **November 13**. The official catalog gives a remediation due date of November 16 and flags the entry for forensic triage. This deadline should be understood in the context of the applicable US federal-agency directive. [CISA KEV data](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json) · [November 14 report](https://securityaffairs.com/203040/hacking/u-s-cisa-adds-check-point-and-unrealircd-flaws-to-its-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog.html)

**Key details:** In an UnrealIRCd network of two or more connected servers, a regular user can inject a server-to-server command that executes **with IRC server privileges**. Maintainers describe the possibility of abusing IRC administrative actions, such as forcibly disconnecting users or disrupting the network. This should not be misrepresented as confirmed operating-system shell command execution or root compromise. [CISA KEV data](https://www.cisa.gov/sites/default/files/feeds/known_exploited_vulnerabilities.json) · [Maintainer announcement](https://forums.unrealircd.org/viewtopic.php?t=9487)

**Why it matters:** The flaw abuses trust between connected IRC servers, so checking only one server is insufficient. **What to do:** Upgrade to **6.2.7.1**, released November 7, or apply the official `bundle-20261107` hot patch to affected 6.0.0–6.2.7 servers and confirm module status. Windows deployments and systems where the hot patch fails must be upgraded. An interim filter workaround requires *every* server to run at least 6.0.4, so check those conditions first. Review patch status and suspicious IRC administrative activity across the connected network. [Maintainer guidance](https://forums.unrealircd.org/viewtopic.php?t=9487)

### 2. SCKit activity through ScreenConnect requires both client updates and extension restrictions

**What's new:** November 14 follow-up reporting discussed Microsoft's **November 13 SCKit analysis**. Microsoft observed attackers abusing ScreenConnect environments run by managed service providers (MSPs) to deploy unauthorized remote-access clients and SCKit to customer endpoints. It also analyzed capabilities intended to spread through technician sessions, without establishing that this route succeeded in every observed case. The initial entry path and the role of extensions remained under investigation. [Microsoft analysis](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/13/sckit-abuses-screenconnect-deploy-remote-access-target-technician-sessions/) · [Follow-up report](https://root2sec.com/threat-intel/sckit-abuses-screenconnect-to-deploy-remote-access-and-target-technician-sessions/)

**Impact and required actions:** ConnectWise's November 13, 21:15 UTC advisory instructs operators to **update all technician clients to version 26.6.5 or later**. The client fix is associated with **CVE-2026-84869**. Separately, self-hosted deployments must disable the **Labtech Plus, RMM Plus, RMM+, RMM Plus Password Link, and RMM+ Password Link** extensions. **ITBoost Integration, RepairShopr Integration, and Guest Session Starter** were also included as precautionary extension-disablement targets. ConnectWise cannot disable these components on customers' self-hosted servers on their behalf. [Official ConnectWise advisory](https://www.connectwise.com/company/trust/advisories)

**What to do:** Verify the actual technician-client versions on technician machines, separately from any cloud-console or server update. Keep the listed extensions disabled pending further guidance. Where compromise is suspected, investigate persistence such as scheduled tasks, unauthorized remote-access applications, and exposed accounts or web sessions. Disabling an extension does not remediate an already compromised host. [ConnectWise advisory](https://www.connectwise.com/company/trust/advisories) · [Microsoft analysis](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/security/blog/2026/11/13/sckit-abuses-screenconnect-deploy-remote-access-target-technician-sessions/)

**Limits of the observations:** In a separate investigation of fake guest sessions named after cryptocurrency exchanges, Huntress found no evidence in those sessions of malicious file transfer, command execution, or delivery of a payload targeting technicians. A connection between that group of sessions and SCKit was also not confirmed. Operators should avoid connecting to unfamiliar sessions and preserve logs and session data before deleting them. Unauthorized sessions, compromise of customer devices, and successful spread to technician devices are distinct findings. [Huntress investigation](https://www.huntress.com/blog/fake-crypto-exchange-sessions-screenconnect)
